# Jonathan Houseley

[Jon Houseley " Babraham Institute](https://www.babraham.ac.uk/our-research/epigenetics/jon-houseley)

Recent work from [Jon Houseley](Jonathan%20Houseley%202f4317f516714fb49acf76d5175c6907.md) shows that highly directed mutations in yeast in response to copper toxicity are in fact adaptive. A series of papers his lab has demonstrated that in response to  copper, an environmental stressor in yeast, cells undergo stimulated copy number amplification of the CUP1 gene, which encodes a protein that sequesters excess copper.  This stimulated copy number variation (CNV) is dependent on the transcription of the gene, and it results in the formation of extra-chromosomal circular DNA elements, which form due to the repetitive nature of the CUP1 locus.  This CNV is likely a result of the interplay between the transcriptional machinery and the DNA replication machinery, as it is dependent on late firing origins of replication present in CUP1 repeats regions.
